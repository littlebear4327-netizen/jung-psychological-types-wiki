# Chapter XI: Definitions

- **Source File**: `raw/C.-G.-Jung-Collected-Works-Volume-6_-Psychological-Types.pdf` (pp. 391–460)
- **TL;DR**: The foundational conceptual dictionary of Analytical Psychology, defining 57 core technical terms (paragraphs 672–844) to overcome the ambiguity of psychological terminology and establish a precise structural, energic, and developmental taxonomy of the psyche.

---

## 1. Problem Statement & Motivation
- **Core Challenge**: Psychology is the youngest of sciences and deals with empirical material that cannot be displayed in concrete, measurable form before the observer's eyes `(p. 391)`. While quantitative methods (e.g., word-association experiments) can measure elementary facts, they cannot do justice to complex psychic phenomena `(p. 391)`.
- **The Ambiguity of Terminology**: Once measurable facts are transcended, psychology depends entirely on concepts. When concepts such as "feeling", "unconscious", or "idea" are vague and ambiguous, mutual understanding becomes impossible `(pp. 391–392)`.
- **Methodological Solution**: Jung sets forth 57 definitions in alphabetical order to establish the precise empirical meaning in which he uses them in his typological and analytical system, serving as a critical standard for psychological orientation `(p. 392)`.

---

## 2. Core Thematic Taxonomy of the 57 Definitions

The 57 definitions form an interlocking architecture that spans four core domains of analytical psychology:

### 2.1 Structural Architecture of the Psyche
1. **Psyche vs. Soul** `(pp. 436, 440)`:
   - `[[concepts/Psyche]]` is the totality of all psychic processes, conscious as well as unconscious `(p. 440)`.
   - `[[concepts/Soul]]` is a demarcated functional complex or "inner personality" `(p. 440)`.
2. **Ego (Ego-Complex)** `(p. 406)`:
   - A complex of ideas constituting the center of the field of consciousness, possessing high continuity and identity `(p. 406)`. It is merely the subject of consciousness, not identical with the total psyche.
3. **Consciousness** `(p. 403)`:
   - The function or activity maintaining the relation of psychic contents to the ego `(p. 403)`. It is selective and discriminating; direction demands the exclusion of irrelevant contents `(p. 401)`.
4. **Persona** `(pp. 442–444)`:
   - The mask or ad hoc adopted attitude toward the external object; the outward face of the personality adapted to social collective demands `(pp. 442–444)`.
5. **Soul-Image (Anima / Animus)** `(pp. 444–448)`:
   - The inward face or attitude turned toward the unconscious `(p. 444)`. In men, it is personified as the feminine `[[concepts/Anima-Animus|Anima]]`; in women, as the masculine `[[concepts/Anima-Animus|Animus]]` `(p. 445)`. Characterized by complementarity to the persona `(pp. 444–445)`.
6. **The Unconscious (Personal & Collective)** `(pp. 458–460)`:
   - Psychological borderline concept denoting all psychic contents not related to the ego `(p. 458)`.
   - **Personal Unconscious**: Acquisitions of personal life, forgotten, repressed, or subliminally perceived `(p. 459)`.
   - **Collective Unconscious**: Inherited brain structure and mythological associations/engrams common to mankind `(p. 460)`.
7. **The Self** `(pp. 437–438)`:
   - The subject of the total psyche (conscious + unconscious), representing psychic totality and unity `(p. 437)`. An empirical postulate and transcendental concept symbolized by mandalas, quaternities, circles, and divine figures `(pp. 437–438)`.

### 2.2 Energetics & Dynamic Regulative Mechanisms
1. **Libido (Psychic Energy)** `(p. 433)`:
   - Quantitative intensity of psychic processes, equivalent to energy `(p. 433)`. Inherent in the concept of energy is polarity: pairs of opposites `(pp. 202, 433)`.
2. **Compensation** `(pp. 400–401)`:
   - The self-regulation of the psychic apparatus. The unconscious acts as a counterweight balancing the one-sidedness of the conscious attitude `(p. 400)`. Extreme conscious one-sidedness produces antagonistic unconscious counterpositions `(p. 401)`.
3. **Enantiodromia** `(pp. 406–407)`:
   - Heraclitean principle: everything that exists runs counter into its opposite `(p. 406)`. When an extreme one-sided conscious attitude prevails, the unconscious counterposition builds up and eventually breaks through `(p. 407)`.
4. **Projection & Introjection** `(pp. 430, 435)`:
   - **Projection**: Expulsion of a subjective content into an object; dissimilation of subject from object `(p. 435)`.
   - **Introjection**: Indrawing of the object into the subjective sphere of interest; assimilation of object to subject `(p. 430)`.
5. **Participation Mystique & Identity** `(pp. 420, 434)`:
   - A priori unconscious oneness or conformity between subject and object, where the boundary between self and other is blurred `(pp. 420, 434)`.
6. **Archaism & Concretism** `(pp. 395, 401–403)`:
   - **Archaism**: Relics of primitive mentality where psychological functions are fused and undifferentiated `(p. 395)`.
   - **Concretism**: Thinking and feeling "grown together" with sensation and physical stimuli, incapable of pure abstraction `(pp. 401–402)`.
7. **Will** `(p. 460)`:
   - Amount of psychic energy at the disposal of consciousness; volition is an energic process released by conscious motivation `(p. 460)`.

### 2.3 Typological & Functional Taxonomy
1. **Psychological Function** `(p. 416)`:
   - A particular form of psychic activity remaining constant under varying conditions `(p. 416)`.
   - **Rational Functions**: `[[concepts/Rational-vs-Irrational-Functions|Thinking & Feeling]]`—evaluative, judging, governed by reflective reason `(pp. 415, 436, 456)`.
   - **Irrational Functions**: `[[concepts/Rational-vs-Irrational-Functions|Sensation & Intuition]]`—perceptive, receptive, oriented to what happens without judgment `(pp. 431–433, 438–440)`.
2. **Attitude & Orientation** `(pp. 396–399, 434)`:
   - **Attitude**: A readiness of the psyche to act or react in a definite direction `(p. 396)`.
   - **Extraversion**: Centrifugal outward-turning of libido toward the object `(p. 407)`.
   - **Introversion**: Centripetal inward-turning of libido toward the subjective factor `(p. 430)`.
3. **Inferior Function** `(pp. 428–429)`:
   - The function lagging behind in differentiation, relatively unconscious, archaic, ambivalent, and prone to autonomous compulsions `(pp. 428–429)`.
4. **Type** `(pp. 457–458)`:
   - Specimen of a habitual attitude (extravert/introvert) combined with a predominant basic function (thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition), yielding 8 basic function-types `(p. 457)`.

### 2.4 Individuation, Symbol Formation & Methodology
1. **Differentiation** `(p. 405)`:
   - Separation of parts from the whole; freeing a function from contamination with other functions so it can become directed `(p. 405)`.
2. **Individuation** `(pp. 426–428)`:
   - The process of psychological differentiation having for its goal the development of the individual personality `(p. 426)`. Does not lead to isolation, but to broader and more intense collective relationships `(p. 427)`. Distinct from pathological individualism `(p. 427)`.
3. **Symbol vs. Sign** `(pp. 449–454)`:
   - **Sign**: Semiotic token standing for a known entity `(pp. 449–450)`.
   - **Symbol**: The best possible formulation of a relatively unknown fact, pregnant with meaning, uniting opposites `(pp. 449–451)`.
4. **Transcendent Function** `(pp. 121, 455–456, 457)`:
   - A complex function combining conscious and unconscious elements that facilitates the transition from one attitude to another through the living symbol `(pp. 121, 455–456)`.
5. **Constructive / Synthetic Method vs. Reductive Method** `(pp. 403–405, 437)`:
   - **Reductive Method** (Freud/Adler): Traces unconscious products back to historical origins, infantile wishes, or elementary instincts `(p. 437)`.
   - **Constructive/Synthetic Method**: Forward-looking (teleological/prospective); elaborates unconscious symbols into a future-oriented developmental meaning `(pp. 403–405)`.
6. **Active vs. Passive Fantasy** `(pp. 408–410)`:
   - **Passive Fantasy**: Involuntary automatisms arising from dissociated unconscious complexes `(p. 408)`.
   - **Active Fantasy**: Evoked by an intuitive attitude uniting conscious intention with unconscious imagery; precursor of Active Imagination `(pp. 408–409)`.

## 4. Critical Takeaways & Theoretical Insights
1. **The Epistemological Triad**:
   - `Esse in re` (objective reality) $\leftrightarrow$ `Esse in intellectu` (logical idea) $\leftrightarrow$ `Esse in anima` (living psychic reality) `(pp. 57–63)`.
   - The symbol is born in *esse in anima*, acting as the creative reconciler between object and concept `(p. 62)`.
2. **Individuation vs. Individualism**:
   - Individuation is natural differentiation of the self from the collective norm; it enriches social cohesion `(p. 427)`.
   - Individualism is pathological self-assertion raising an individual peculiarity into an absolute norm `(p. 427)`.
3. **The Transcendent Function as Dynamic Engine**:
   - Arises when conscious will is paralyzed by equal opposition between thesis and antithesis `(p. 454)`.
   - Energy regresses into the unconscious, activating primordial images and generating the uniting symbol `(pp. 454–455)`.
