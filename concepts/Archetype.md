# Archetype (Primordial Image)

## Definition & Foundational Premise
The **Archetype**—termed by C. G. Jung in *Psychological Types* primarily as the **Primordial Image** (*Urbild*) or **Archetypal Image**—is an innate, structural component of the `[[papers/CH11-Definitions|collective unconscious]]` `(pp. 421–425)`:
- Jung defines the primordial image as "a mnemic deposit, an engram (Semon), which has arisen through the condensation of countless processes of a similar kind" `(p. 422)`.
- It represents the psychic residue of millions of years of ancestral human experience: "It is the psychic expression of an anatomically, physiologically determined disposition" `(p. 423)`.
- **Inherited Form vs. Inherited Content**: Jung explicitly insists that archetypes are **not** inherited ideas or specific mental representations; they are inherited *modes of functioning*, structural possibilities, or a priori formal preconditions for psychological apprehension `(pp. 422–424)`. They function in the psyche much like the categories of understanding in Kantian epistemology or the axial system of a crystal `(pp. 377, 423)`.

## Characteristics & Epistemic Attributes
Jung identifies five core criteria defining the primordial image `(pp. 421–425)`:

1. **Archaic Quality**: It bears a distinctly archaic, mythological character, corresponding closely to primitive myths, folk tales, and religious motifs `(pp. 395, 422)`.
2. **Autonomous Affective Energy**: Archetypes possess a high degree of numinosity and independent libido. When activated, they exert a compelling, fascinating, or terrifying influence upon conscious ideation `(pp. 423–424)`.
3. **Universality**: Because they are rooted in the universal morphology of the human brain, they appear across all cultures, historical epochs, and individuals, independently of cultural transmission `(pp. 422–423)`.
4. **Relativity to Experience**: An archetype remains a latent, virtual disposition until an external life situation evokes it. Once triggered by concrete historical experience, it clothes itself in contemporary empirical imagery `(pp. 423–425)`.
5. **Polarity of Meaning**: Every archetype contains a dual aspect (light and dark, constructive and destructive, sublime and terrible) `(pp. 422, 451–453)`.

## Archetypes in Typology & Cognitive Functions
- **Introverted Intuition ($Ni$)**: The primary cognitive channel perceiving archetypal forms directly is `[[methods/Introverted-Intuition|Introverted Intuition]]` `(pp. 362–365)`. While extraverted intuition chases outer possibilities, introverted intuition perceives the unfolding dynamics of primordial images within the collective unconscious `(p. 362)`.
- **Subjective Factor**: The primordial image constitutes the innermost structural foundation of the `[[concepts/Subjective-Factor|Subjective Factor]]`, providing the eternal matrix through which all introverted functions interpret empirical reality `(pp. 376–378, 423)`.
- **Contamination with Archaism**: In undifferentiated or inferior functions, archetypal energy contaminates conscious contents, producing paranoid projections, archaic superstitions, or compulsive dogmatism `(pp. 326–328, 364–365, 428–429)`.

## Major Structural Archetypes in *Psychological Types*
Throughout Chapter XI and the volume, Jung delineates several paramount archetypes that govern the individuation journey:
- **The Shadow**: The repressed, inferior, and archaic dark side of the personality `(pp. 400–401, 428–429)`.
- **The Soul-Image**: The contrasexual inner personality archetype—`[[concepts/Anima-Animus|Anima]]` in men and `[[concepts/Anima-Animus|Animus]]` in women `(pp. 444–448)`.
- **The Persona**: The functional archetype of social adaptation and mask `(pp. 441–444)`.
- **The Self**: The central archetype of order, wholeness, and psychic totality `(pp. 437–438)`.
- **The Uniting Symbol**: The archetypal synthesis resolving the warfare of opposites `(pp. 449–456)`.
