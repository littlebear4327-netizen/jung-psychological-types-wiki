# General Description of the Types (Chapter X)

- **Source File**: `raw/C.-G.-Jung-Collected-Works-Volume-6_-Psychological-Types.pdf` (pp. 319–390)
- **TL;DR**: Jung systematically formulates his complete psychological typology, establishing two overarching attitude-types ([[Introversion]] and [[Extraversion]]) governed by the direction of libido relative to subject and object, crossed with four basic psychological functions (Thinking, Feeling, Sensation, Intuition), yielding eight distinct function-types regulated by unconscious compensation and the auxiliary function mechanism.

---

## 1. Problem Statement & Motivation
- **Core Challenge**: Resolving the chaotic multiplicity of individual human differences and conflicting psychological/philosophical theories by identifying the structural, typical regularities of human consciousness and unconscious compensation `(pp. 319–321, 388–390)`.
- **Key Flaw of Prior Work**:
  - Academic psychology and psychiatry either reduced individual differences to superficial temperaments (Galen's 4 humours), crude behavior, or physiological "brain mythology" `(p. 320)`.
  - Psychoanalytic theories made one-sided universal claims: Freud constructed an essentially extraverted, reductive, objective-instinct psychology, while Adler constructed an introverted, ego-superiority, finalistic psychology `(p. 347)`. Each type took its own subjective premise as an absolute norm and dismissed the opposite type as pathological or inferior `(pp. 346–347, 360–361)`.

---

## 2. Structural Architecture & Core Mechanisms

### 2.1 The Two Attitude-Types (Movement of Libido)
- **[[Extraversion]]**:
  - *Mechanism*: Centrifugal movement of libido; positive relation and unconditional orientation towards the external object `(pp. 319, 322)`. The object acts as a decisive determinant for decisions, judgments, and actions `(p. 322)`.
  - *Conscious Danger*: Extreme absorption in the object leads to complete suppression of the subjective factor and bodily/psychic alienation `(pp. 324–325)`.
  - *Unconscious Compensation*: The unconscious develops an infantile, archaic, egocentric, and highly subjective counterposition `(pp. 326–328)`. The neurosis par excellence is **hysteria** `(p. 325)`.
- **[[Introversion]]**:
  - *Mechanism*: Centripetal movement of libido; withdrawal of energy from the object into the subject `(pp. 319, 359)`. Perception and judgment are interlinked with and determined by the **subjective factor** (the a priori archetypal disposition of the collective unconscious) `(pp. 360–362)`.
  - *Conscious Danger*: Devaluation of the object and confusion of the ego with the self, producing megalomania or defensive alienation `(pp. 363–364)`.
  - *Unconscious Compensation*: The repressed relation to the object takes on an archaic, infantile, magical, and terrifying character; the object becomes endowed with overwhelming, sinister power `(pp. 364–365)`. The neurosis par excellence is **psychasthenia** (obsessional fatigue and anxiety) `(p. 364)`.

### 2.2 The Four Basic Functions & The Rational / Irrational Division
Jung establishes four basic functions configured along two orthogonal axes `(pp. 346–348, 356–357, 388–390)`:
1. **Rational (Judging) Functions**: Operate through reflective evaluation and principles according to laws of reason.
   - **Thinking**: Conceptual connection and intellectual cognition `(pp. 330–334, 365–368)`.
   - **Feeling**: Subjective valuation of acceptance vs rejection ("like" / "dislike") `(pp. 341–343, 371–373)`.
   - *Mutual Exclusivity*: Pure thinking strictly excludes feeling, and differentiated feeling strictly excludes thinking `(pp. 344, 389)`.
2. **Irrational (Perceiving) Functions**: Operate through sheer perception of the flux of events beyond rational judgment.
   - **Sensation**: Perception mediated through sensory organs; establishes *what is actually present* (fonction du réel) `(pp. 348–352, 377–380)`.
   - **Intuition**: Perception via the unconscious; establishes *hidden possibilities and future trajectories* `(pp. 352–356, 382–386)`.
   - *Mutual Exclusivity*: Sensation is anchored to physical actualities, while intuition requires suppression of sensory stimuli to perceive emergent unconscious possibilities `(pp. 352–353, 382)`.

### 2.3 The Principle of the Auxiliary Function
- In practice, no individual exists in pure one-function form; such an individual would be a caricature or a psychological invalid `(p. 388)`.
- **Rule of Combination**: The dominant (primary) differentiated function is paired with a secondary, auxiliary function whose nature is **different from, but not antagonistic to**, the dominant function `(pp. 388–389)`.
  - Rational dominant (Thinking or Feeling) can only pair with an Irrational auxiliary (Sensation or Intuition) `(p. 389)`.
  - Irrational dominant (Sensation or Intuition) can only pair with a Rational auxiliary (Thinking or Feeling) `(p. 389)`.
  - Two functions of the same axis (Thinking + Feeling, or Sensation + Intuition) can never coexist at equal conscious strength without reducing both to an undifferentiated, archaic state `(p. 389)`.

## 4. Critical Takeaways & Theoretical Implications
1. **Typology is a Critical Apparatus, Not a Parlour Game**: Jung vigorously refutes using typology to stick superficial labels on people `(p. 15, Foreword to Argentine edn; pp. 388, 463–464)`. It serves as a coordinate system (a trigonometric compass) to map psychic reality and determine the observer's "personal equation" `(p. 522)`.
2. **The Dynamic Complementarity of Consciousness and the Unconscious**: The conscious attitude and the unconscious counterposition stand in a dynamic, compensatory equilibrium `(pp. 326, 363, 390)`. If consciousness becomes overly one-sided, the unconscious counter-function breaks through in archaic, destructive symptoms `(pp. 327–328, 364)`.
3. **The Subjective Factor as Inborn Reality**: Jung defends the validity of the subjective factor against 19th-century positivism and empiricism: the mind is not a *tabula rasa*; the brain carries an ancestral architecture of preformed modes of apprehension (the `[[concepts/Archetype|Archetype / Primordial Image]]`) `(pp. 361–362)`.
4. **Differentiation Requires the Auxiliary Channel**: An analyst or individual cannot jump directly from the superior function to the most repressed inferior function across the oppositional axis; development must progress through the auxiliary function `(p. 390)`.
