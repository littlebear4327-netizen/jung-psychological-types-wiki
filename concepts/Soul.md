# Soul (*Seele*)

## Definition & Foundational Premise
The **Soul** (*Seele*) is defined by C. G. Jung not as a theological or metaphysical substance, but as a clearly demarcated functional complex or "inner personality" within the human psyche `(pp. 440–444)`:
- "By the soul I understand a clearly demarcated functional complex that can best be described as a 'personality'" `(p. 440)`.
- While the **Persona** represents the outer personality or outward attitude adapted toward the external world and social collective, the **Soul** represents the inner personality or inward attitude turned toward the unconscious `(pp. 441, 444)`.

## The Complementary Polarity of Persona and Soul
Jung formulates the relationship between Persona and Soul as an exact reciprocal, compensatory balance `(pp. 444–446)`:
1. **Opposite Qualities**:
   - The qualities of the soul are regularly the opposite of the persona `(p. 445)`.
   - If an individual possesses a hard, intellectual, and unyielding outer persona, their inner soul is soft, sensitive, sentimental, and rebellious `(p. 445)`.
   - If an individual presents a gentle, yielding, and modest outer persona, their inner soul is ruthless, tyrannical, and obstinate `(p. 445)`.
2. **The Soul-Image**:
   - The soul is personified in the unconscious as the contrasexual archetype: the `[[concepts/Anima-Animus|Anima]]` in a man and the `[[concepts/Anima-Animus|Animus]]` in a woman `(pp. 444–448)`.
   - When unrecognized, the soul is projected onto external love-objects or enemies, causing intense fascinations, destructive dependencies, or bitter resentments `(pp. 446–448)`.

## Pathology of Repression & Identification
- **Identification with the Soul**: If the conscious ego identifies with the soul, the individual loses contact with outer reality, becoming pathologically moody, possessed by unconscious complexes, eccentric, or psychotic `(pp. 443–444)`.
- **Repression of the Soul**: If the individual identifies totally with the persona, repressing the soul, the soul regresses into the archaic unconscious, eventually executing an autonomous ambush via neurosis or `[[concepts/Enantiodromia|enantiodromia]]` `(pp. 406–407, 444–446)`.

## Role in Individuation
- Differentiating the conscious ego from both the outer persona and the inner soul is a crucial prerequisite for `[[concepts/Individuation]]` `(pp. 426–428, 444)`.
- Only when both complexes are consciously recognized can the psyche achieve balance, activate the `[[concepts/Transcendent-Function|transcendent function]]`, and progress toward the `[[concepts/Self]]` `(pp. 437–438, 455–456)`.
