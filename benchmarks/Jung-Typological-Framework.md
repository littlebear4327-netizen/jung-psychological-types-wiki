# Jung's Typological Framework (The 8-Type Coordinate System)

## Overview & Scientific Intent
**Jung's Typological Framework** is a systematic structural matrix designed to map individual psychic differences, theoretical dissensions in psychology, and the dynamics of conscious-unconscious compensation `(pp. 319–390, 463–464)`. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Typology is a Critical Apparatus, Not a Diagnostic Label**: Jung repeatedly warned against using his typology as a parlour game or superficial labeling system `(p. 15, Foreword to Argentine edn; pp. 388, 463–464)`. It was conceived as a critical apparatus—a psychological coordinate system (or trigonometric compass) enabling the psychologist to account for their own "personal equation" when evaluating others `(pp. 463–464, 522)`.

---

## 1. The Dimensional Coordinates

The framework is constructed along three orthogonal structural dimensions:
1. **Attitude Dimension (Libido Direction)**:
   - **[[concepts/Extraversion]]**: Centrifugal, object-determined, expansive adaptation `(pp. 319–328)`.
   - **[[concepts/Introversion]]**: Centripetal, subject-determined via the [[concepts/Subjective-Factor]] `(pp. 359–365)`.
2. **Operational Axis Dimension**:
   - **Rational (Judging)**: Governed by reason, reflection, and values (**Thinking** vs **Feeling**) `(pp. 346–348, 454–455)`.
   - **Irrational (Perceiving)**: Governed by sheer perception of events (**Sensation** vs **Intuition**) `(pp. 356–357, 438–440)`.
3. **Conscious-Unconscious Polarity**:
   - The conscious superior function is counterbalanced by a compensatory, undifferentiated, and archaic inferior function in the unconscious `(pp. 326–328, 363–365, 388–390)`.

## 3. The 16 Empirical Functional Combinations
By applying the **[[methods/Auxiliary-Function]] Principle** `(pp. 388–390)`, each dominant function pairs with a non-antagonistic auxiliary function from the opposite axis:

```
[Dominant: Rational (Te / Ti / Fe / Fi)]  +  [Auxiliary: Irrational (Se / Si / Ne / Ni)]
[Dominant: Irrational (Se / Si / Ne / Ni)] +  [Auxiliary: Rational (Te / Ti / Fe / Fi)]
```

Empirically yielding 16 distinct functional configurations:
1. **$Te$ with auxiliary $Si$** (Practical administrative organizer)
2. **$Te$ with auxiliary $Ni$** (Strategic theoretical organizer)
3. **$Fe$ with auxiliary $Si$** (Social caretaker / cultural preserver)
4. **$Fe$ with auxiliary $Ni$** (Inspiring charismatic educator / counselor)
5. **$Ti$ with auxiliary $Se$** (Pragmatic technical analyst / craftsman)
6. **$Ti$ with auxiliary $Ne$** (Theoretical conceptual system builder)
7. **$Fi$ with auxiliary $Se$** (Artistic sensory craftsman / quiet moralist)
8. **$Fi$ with auxiliary $Ne$** (Poetic idealistic creator / advocate)
9. **$Se$ with auxiliary $Ti$** (Pragmatic crisis-solver / concrete realist)
10. **$Se$ with auxiliary $Fi$** (Aesthetic sensory connoisseur / performer)
11. **$Ne$ with auxiliary $Ti$** (Inventive entrepreneurial strategist)
12. **$Ne$ with auxiliary $Fi$** (Visionary humanitarian champion)
13. **$Si$ with auxiliary $Te$** (Methodical institutional steward)
14. **$Si$ with auxiliary $Fe$** (Loyal community stabilizer)
15. **$Ni$ with auxiliary $Te$** (Visionary system architect / reformer)
16. **$Ni$ with auxiliary $Fe$** (Insightful psychotherapist / spiritual guide)

## 4. Comparison: Jung’s Dynamic Model vs Post-Jungian Derivatives

| Structural Dimension | C. G. Jung (CW 6, Original Model) | Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) | Socionics (Model A) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Purpose** | Coordinate system for mapping psychic energy, shadow compensation, and the personal equation `(pp. 15, 463)` | Static personality categorization for vocational guidance and team dynamics | Informational metabolism model for interpersonal compatibility |
| **Conscious / Unconscious** | Dynamic compensatory balance: superior conscious function has an archaic unconscious polar opposite `(pp. 326, 363)` | Conscious focus; shadow functions largely relegated to auxiliary/tertiary/inferior without deep archetypal compensation | 8 distinct positions divided into conscious mental ring and unconscious vital ring |
| **Attitude of Auxiliary** | Auxiliary is subservient to dominant; its attitude is flexible and non-antagonistic `(pp. 388–389)` | Strictly opposite attitude to dominant (e.g. $Te$ dominant has $Ni$ or $Si$ auxiliary) | Explicitly alternates attitude across all 8 functions |
| **Inferior Function State** | Archaic, undifferentiated, infantile, highly contaminated with the collective unconscious `(pp. 388–390)` | "Grip" state or aspiration, but rarely treated as an autonomous archaic archetypal danger | 4th function ("point of least resistance", PoLR) is vulnerable and painful |
| **Individuation Teleology** | Resolving polar tension via the [[concepts/Transcendent-Function]] to realize the [[concepts/Self]] `(pp. 458–460)` | Self-understanding and optimizing natural preferences | Social role fulfillment and dualization |
