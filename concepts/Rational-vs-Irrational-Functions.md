# Rational vs. Irrational Functions

## Definition & Functional Division
In Chapter X and Chapter XI of *Psychological Types*, Jung divides the four basic psychological functions into two distinct, orthogonal categories based on how they process psychic contents `(pp. 346–348, 356–357, 388–390, 438–440, 454–455)`:
1. **Rational (Judging) Functions**: **Thinking** and **Feeling** `(pp. 454–455)`.
2. **Irrational (Perceiving) Functions**: **Sensation** and **Intuition** `(pp. 438–440)`.


## 1. Rational (Judging) Functions: Thinking & Feeling
- **Core Definition**: Rational functions operate by **judgment and reflective evaluation** in accordance with the laws of reason, objective norms, or cohesive principles `(pp. 346–347, 454–455)`.
- **Reason Defined**: Reason is not limited to intellect; it is the human capacity to establish harmony, coherence, and order based on values and principles `(pp. 454–455)`.
  - **Thinking**: Evaluates via conceptual connection; answers the question: *"What is this thing conceptually, and how does it logically connect?"* `(pp. 330–334, 365–368, 458–459)`.
  - **Feeling**: Evaluates via subjective valuation; answers the question: *"What is the value or worth of this thing to human life/society?"* (acceptance vs rejection) `(pp. 341–343, 371–373, 428–430)`.
- **Antagonistic Opposition**: Thinking and Feeling stand on the same judging axis in direct polar opposition. In conscious differentiation, they are mutually exclusive: pure logical analysis shatters feeling valuation, and feeling attachment blinds objective intellectual judgment `(pp. 344, 389)`.

---

## 2. Irrational (Perceiving) Functions: Sensation & Intuition
- **Core Definition**: Irrational functions operate by **sheer perception** of actual occurrences without evaluation, moral judgment, or logical reasoning `(pp. 356–357, 438–440)`.
- **"Irrational" Clarification**: Jung explicitly emphasizes that "irrational" does *not* mean "unreasonable" or "anti-rational". It means **non-rational** (*außervernünftig*)—operating outside or beyond the jurisdiction of reason, grounded simply in the sheer, uncalculated fact that things exist and happen `(pp. 356, 438–439)`.
  - **Sensation**: Perception of physical, concrete reality through the sense organs; establishes *what is actually present* (the *fonction du réel*) `(pp. 348–351, 377–380, 452–453)`.
  - **Intuition**: Perception of hidden possibilities, trajectories, and symbolic patterns via the unconscious; establishes *where things come from and where they are going* `(pp. 352–356, 382–386, 437–438)`.
- **Antagonistic Opposition**: Sensation and Intuition stand on the same perceiving axis in direct polar opposition. Sensation requires immersion in present physical stimuli, whereas intuition requires detachment from physical sensations to perceive nascent unconscious possibilities `(pp. 352–353, 382, 389)`.

---

## 3. Structural Laws of Combination
As formalized in `[[methods/Auxiliary-Function]]`, consciousness cannot sustain two functions from the same axis at equal conscious strength `(pp. 388–390)`:
- A Rational dominant function (Thinking or Feeling) can only form a functional, differentiated partnership with an Irrational auxiliary function (Sensation or Intuition) `(p. 389)`.
- An Irrational dominant function (Sensation or Intuition) can only form a functional partnership with a Rational auxiliary function (Thinking or Feeling) `(p. 389)`.
