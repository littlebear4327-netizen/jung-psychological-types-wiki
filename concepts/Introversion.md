# Introversion

## Definition & Core Mechanism
**Introversion** is an overarching psychological attitude characterized by a centripetal movement of [[Libido]] (psychic energy) withdrawing from the external object toward the subject `(pp. 319, 359, 437)`. In an introverted attitude:
- The subject does not relate directly to the raw object, but interposes the inner **[[Subjective-Factor]]** (the ancestral, archetypal disposition of the psyche) between perception and action `(pp. 360–362)`.
- Libido flows inward, prioritizing inner subjective processes, ideals, structures, and inner realities over external events `(pp. 359–361)`.
- The subject selects, evaluates, and filters all objective phenomena through the prism of internal subjective necessity `(pp. 360–363)`.

## Conscious Manifestation & Defense Mechanisms
- **Subjective Sovereignty**: The introvert feels constantly pressured by the demands of the outer world and defends their independence by setting up barriers, theories, or moral reserves against outer encroachment `(pp. 362–364)`.
- **Confusion of Ego and Self**: A primary hazard of the introvert is confusing the personal ego with the supra-personal [[Self]], resulting in intellectual arrogance, aloofness, or megalomanic fantasy `(pp. 363–364)`.
- **Devaluation of the Object**: To protect inner sovereignty, the introvert tends to deprecate, minimize, or intellectualize the external world and other people `(pp. 363–365)`.

## Unconscious Compensation & Pathology
- **The Compensatory Law**: The more the conscious mind minimizes and withdraws from the object, the more the unconscious attaches itself to the object in an archaic, magical, and terrifying manner `(pp. 364–365)`.
- **The Archaic Object**: The repressed object relation takes on sinister, demonic qualities. The introvert imagines the object possesses overwhelming power, secret malevolence, or irresistible fascination `(pp. 364–365)`.
- **Characteristic Neurosis**: **Psychasthenia** (*psychasthénie*—Janet) is the neurosis par excellence of the introvert `(p. 364)`. It manifests as extreme psychic fatigue, obsessional rituals, agoraphobia, and crippling anxiety over social interaction and outer demands `(pp. 364–365)`.
