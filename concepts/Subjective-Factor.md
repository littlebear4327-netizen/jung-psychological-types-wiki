# The Subjective Factor

## Definition & Epistemological Stance
The **Subjective Factor** (*der subjektive Faktor*) is Jung’s foundational psychological and epistemological concept designating the inborn, a priori psychological disposition of the human subject `(pp. 360–363)`. Jung forcefully defends the subjective factor against 19th-century positivism, naïve realism, and the philosophical dogma of the *tabula rasa* `(pp. 361–362)`:
- The human mind is not a blank slate passively receiving objective sensory impressions `(p. 361)`.
- The human brain inherits an ancient, preformed functional architecture—a phylogenetic precipitate of millions of years of ancestral human experience `(pp. 361–362)`.
- Therefore, every psychological act of perception and judgment is co-determined by two equally real factors: the **objective factor** (the outer stimulus) and the **subjective factor** (the inner archetypal mode of apprehension) `(pp. 360–361)`.

## Role in Typology & Introversion
- **The Engine of Introversion**: While the extravert unconditionally privileges the objective factor, the introvert orients consciousness primarily through the subjective factor `(pp. 360–363)`.
- **Subjective vs Egocentric**: Jung draws a critical distinction: the subjective factor is **not** the arbitrary whim or personal conceit of the ego. It is a supra-personal, collective reality—the structural matrix of the **[[concepts/Archetype|Archetype / Primordial Image]]** `(pp. 361–362, 421–425)`.
- **Epistemological Equality**: The subjective factor has the same objective validity and evolutionary grounding as the laws of physics that govern the outer environment `(pp. 361–362)`.

## Manifestation Across the Introverted Functions
In `[[papers/CH10-General-Description-of-the-Types]]`, the subjective factor conditions all four introverted functions:
1. **[[methods/Introverted-Thinking]] ($Ti$)**: Develops thoughts not from objective facts, but from subjective primordial ideas rooted in the collective unconscious `(pp. 365–367)`.
2. **[[methods/Introverted-Feeling]] ($Fi$)**: Values objects according to non-verbal archetypal ideals and inner spiritual depth `(pp. 371–374)`.
3. **[[methods/Introverted-Sensation]] ($Si$)**: Interposes an archaic, mythic patina between the physical stimulus and sensory perception `(pp. 377–380)`.
4. **[[methods/Introverted-Intuition]] ($Ni$)**: Directs perception toward the autonomous unfolding of inner archetypal images `(pp. 382–385)`.

## Pathological Distortion
When an introverted individual lacks self-awareness, the ego mistakenly identifies with the subjective factor `(pp. 363–364)`. The person believes that the archetypal power belongs to their personal ego, producing intellectual megalomania, solipsism, arrogant alienation from society, and neurotic terror of the object `(pp. 363–365)`.
